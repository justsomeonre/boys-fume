The Search Across Three Continents.

In early 2024, our founding creative committee set out to launch a modern olfactory titan. We hired perfumers who whispered in French about Bulgarian damask roses harvested under crescent moons. We commissioned industrial designers in Milan to engineer magnetic zinc-alloy caps that produced an audible “thud” of prestige.

We analyzed cardamom from Guatemala, oud from Assam, and synthetic aldehydes that promised to smell like “unspoken ambition on an autumn balcony in Geneva.”

Then we calculated the retail price: ₹14,999 for 50ml of liquid that would vanish from your skin before lunch.

Looking Six Feet to the Left.

During a tense board meeting in Delhi where our spreadsheet insisted we cut customer service to fund a billboard in Bandra, our lead copywriter excused himself to the bathroom.

He turned the stainless chrome tap. Clean, cool municipal water flowed freely. He cupped his hands, took a generous splash across his face, and exhaled.

His skin felt alive. His mind felt clear. His blood pressure dropped. And he didn't owe a French conglomerate a single rupee.

He walked back into the boardroom and made the announcement: “We don't need Grasse. We need the sink.”

“We sell the idea, not the water. Because confidence shouldn't require an EMI option.”
